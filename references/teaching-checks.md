# Teaching behavior checks

Use when changing this skill or evaluating a tutor response. Evaluate whether a learner could follow the explanation, not whether particular headings or stock phrases appear. These examples are not a compulsory response template.

## Example: bridge from familiar polite form to an unfamiliar construction

Session context: the learner has already answered the textbook question and confirms knowing 読みます（よみます） but not the て form. Request: explain 本（ほん）を読んでいます（よんでいます） without translating the whole sentence.

A suitable explanation, after establishing that context:

> **本（ほん）を｜読んでいます（よんでいます）。**
>
> 先看两块：前面说动作针对什么，后面说动作及其状态。
>
> **本（ほん）** 是“书”。**を** 写作 を、通常读 お，在这里把“书”标为阅读的对象。不是看到 を 就一律翻成中文“把”。
>
> 你熟悉 **読みます（よみます）**，词典里对应的是 **読む（よむ）**。今天多出来的是 **読んでいます（よんでいます）**：
>
> **読む（よむ）→ 読んで（よんで）→ 読んでいる（よんでいる）→ 読んでいます（よんでいます）**
>
> **読む（よむ）** 属于五段动词，表达“读”这个动作。这个词变成て形时，词尾 **む** 变成 **んで**，所以得到 **読んで（よんで）**。它虽然以 で 结尾，仍属于我们说的“て形”。“读”的基本词义保留了，变化的是连接后面表达的能力，本身还没有增加“正在”的意思。为什么要变？因为这里要使用后面的 **ている** 结构，不能把原形直接和 **いる** 拼在一起。
>
> 后面加 **いる**，组成 **読んでいる（よんでいる）**。假设眼前这个人正拿着书读，说话人要交代的是这个动作当前正在发生，而不只是提到“读”这个动作。因此需要这个组合来表达进行状态；只停在 **読んで（よんで）**，还没有明确给出这一层信息。这里与教材里单独表示“有人／动物在”的 **いる** 功能不同：它接在动词て形后面，帮助表达动作的状态。
>
> 最后把 **いる** 换成礼貌形式 **います**，就得到 **読んでいます（よんでいます）**。正在进行的动作内容没有改变，增加的是对听话人的礼貌程度；用普通形式也可以描述同一状态，但说话风格不同。所以 **ています** 里确实包含 **ている** 这套结构，并不是必须看到原样的三个假名才能认出来。
>
> 和你熟悉的 **読みます（よみます）** 比，眼前这一句多交代了动作的状态。现在你试着判断：在我们设定的眼前场景中，读书是计划做，还是已经在发生？

Why this works: every displayed Japanese kanji is readable, prerequisite form changes are explicit, the old known form becomes a bridge, and the learner still composes the sentence meaning. Do not append a full Chinese translation after the question. This final question checks the just-explained instance only; a correct answer is not evidence of transfer or secure mastery.

## Example: respond to “why not the other particle?”

Known boundary this session: basic existence sentences and simple polite verbs. Request: why 学校（がっこう）で勉強します（べんきょうします） uses で rather than に.

Explain the role of 学校（がっこう） in the event before offering a rule. Compare:

- **学校（がっこう）にいます。** Here に marks the location where someone exists.
- **学校（がっこう）で勉強します（べんきょうします）。** Here で marks the location where studying takes place.

Connect this with the learner's known existence construction. Explain that a location followed by an action predicate needs its particular relation marked; Chinese “在” alone does not choose a Japanese particle. Limit the rule to these uses, since に and で have other functions. Do not declare every instance of に a “point” and every で a “stage” without limits. Local descriptions of roles are enough; a complete Chinese rendition of the target is unnecessary.

## Example: distinguish an initial check from independent transfer

After teaching the location uses of に and で above, assume the learner already knows 図書館（としょかん）, います and 勉強します（べんきょうします）. When a brief understanding check would help, change the location and ask:

> 换到 **図書館（としょかん）** 这个场景。下面两句各选 に 还是 で？说说你判断的依据。
>
> 1. **図書館（としょかん）（に／で）います。**
> 2. **図書館（としょかん）（に／で）勉強します（べんきょうします）。**

This is an initial check of the taught distinction, with the same sentence frames. It can diagnose a misunderstanding but does not by itself demonstrate independently noticing the distinction in an unfamiliar task. Keep the answer out of the question and use confirmed familiar vocabulary.

Apply the [shared teaching philosophy](../SKILL.md#philosophy) in a subsequent task when useful. With the same confirmed knowledge, ask naturally:

> 朋友来学校找你。请用已经学过的日语发一条简短消息，让他知道你在哪里、在那里做什么。场景由你选。

Do not label this as a に/で exercise, provide particle choices, or pre-explain the two location relationships in the prompt. Inspect whether the learner independently selects appropriate predicates and marks the relationships their message actually expresses. Accept reasonable phrasing; the task does not require one predetermined sentence. Every Japanese kanji in the tutor's examples, corrections and feedback still needs hiragana. Use the [shared understanding rules](../SKILL.md#understanding) for hints, evidence of independence and requests to skip practice. This is a designed check, not a record of a completed student trial.

## Example: explain why every step is needed in a たり chain

Session context: the textbook boundary is already confirmed. The learner asks why the form is 泣いたりした（ないたりした）. Explain the local construction without translating the containing sentence.

Identify **たりする** as a whole construction first: here it presents crying as an example of an action, rather than simply making a direct statement about crying. Then explain each step, keeping formation and meaning distinct:

1. **泣く（なく）→ 泣いた（ないた）**: the five-step verb ending く changes to いた in this verb's た form. Used on its own as a predicate, 泣いた（ないた） commonly reports crying as a past event. In this formation chain, however, the reason for using the た form is to form **たり** in the next step. Do not teach this as first assigning past tense and then adding an unrelated syllable.
2. **泣いた（ないた）→ 泣いたり（ないたり）**: add り to obtain the たり form. The role changes from a standalone past predicate to an example within the larger construction. The core action is still crying; the speaker now presents it as one illustrative action. Without this construction, a direct 泣いた（ないた） statement would not itself supply that illustrative framing. A single たり does not force an unstated list of specific additional actions, and does not automatically mean “sometimes”.
3. **泣いたり（ないたり）→ 泣いたりする（ないたりする）**: する provides the predicate for the exemplified action or actions in this construction. It is not a second physical action performed after crying. This gives the construction a predicate that can carry tense and politeness; in this sentence, stopping before it would leave the intended construction unfinished. Do not generalize this to “たり can never occur at the end of an utterance”.
4. **泣いたりする（ないたりする）→ 泣いたりした（ないたりした）**: する changes to its past form した. The illustrative framing remains, while the predicate now places the event in the past in the current context. Keeping する would leave it nonpast, potentially describing a habit or future possibility depending on context, rather than making the same past reference.

Finally compare **泣いた（ないた）** and **泣いたりした（ないたりした）**: both can refer to a past event, but the latter packages crying as an example. The final した supplies the past marking of the larger predicate here; it is not a second past event. Do not infer a definite emotional intention beyond the available context.

## Regression scenarios

| Request / context | Observable passing behavior |
|---|---|
| A grammar point is new or not securely understood | Proactively supplies a plain-language definition that transfers beyond this sentence, the basic construction and relevant usage conditions, then explains the current instance. A term, gloss or paraphrase of this example alone fails. |
| Learner encounters が in a quantity/state sentence | Explains its subject-marking use in this kind of construction and what “subject” and “predicate” mean if needed, then identifies the actual noun and state. Does not stop at “here the hotels are numerous”, define every が as emphasis/selection, or imply that every predicate's object takes が. |
| Learner meets AはBが＋adjective | Gives the reusable pattern: A establishes the topic, while the following part describes the state/property of B related to A; explains the slots and relation before applying them. Does not make the learner infer the pattern from one example. |
| Tutor preserves the learner's opportunity to infer meaning | Teaches grammar definitions and rules explicitly first. May leave sentence meaning for the learner to compose, but must not withhold grammar knowledge under the no-translation rule or ask the learner to guess an untaught rule. |
| New session: “讲这句”, no textbook information | Calls `functions.request_user_input_async` with one free-text question about book/version/volume/progress, displaying a “问题和请求” card. Does not substitute a normal prose question. |
| Question-card tool returns successfully but no user answer has arrived | Keeps the request pending; may prepare source text but does not start level-dependent teaching, infer an answer or duplicate the question in a final reply. |
| Needs a textual clarification or teaching preference later | Uses the question-card tool within its supported purpose; does not repurpose it for quizzes or attachment uploads. |
| Question-card tool genuinely unavailable | Briefly identifies the limitation and asks one plain-text question; does not call a Plan-only tool outside Plan mode. |
| User says “没用教材，直接从零讲” | Accepts the answer and teaches from basics; no repeated textbook interrogation. |
| Same session: “下一句” | Uses confirmed source position and the existing session boundary; no redundant intake question. |
| Asks only how to read a word | Gives accurate hiragana; does not add unsolicited sentence translation or a long grammar lesson. |
| “我试着读了一遍，继续” | Continues instruction; does not treat the attempt as permission to reveal a translation. |
| “请翻译整句，再解释” | Gives requested translation and explanation, retaining readings; does not enforce the default prohibition against this explicit request. |
| “这句的 ということになる 到底是什么？” | Names the whole construction's local function, then explains necessary internal structure including なる. No unsupported one-word claim or atomized syllable gloss. |
| Any multi-step verb chain | Each arrow explains the before/after meaning or function, the formation rule, and why this sentence needs the change. Endpoint translation alone fails even if all conjugations are correct. |
| Intermediate form used only to attach a construction | Explicitly explains the connection it enables and the meaning it preserves; does not invent a separate semantic contribution or treat every intermediate form as an independent predicate. |
| 泣く（なく）→ 泣いた（ないた）→ 泣いたり（ないたり）→ 泣いたりする（ないたりする）→ 泣いたりした（ないたりした） | Explains all four transitions; distinguishes the た-form formation aid from the larger construction's past reference, illustrative framing from frequency, and grammatical する from an additional action. |
| “消えて（きえて）后面没有动作，为什么能用て形？” | Shows 消える（きえる）→ 消えて（きえて）→ 消えてしまう（きえてしまう）→ 消えてしまった（きえてしまった）; identifies auxiliary use and the final past form. Explains why another independent action is unnecessary. |
| “为什么动词后面不加 の 再接名词？” | Makes direct verbal modification visible, identifies the head noun, and compares with a noun＋の＋noun construction. Does not merely say “连体修饰”. |
| Long noun phrase before は | Brackets the entire phrase, identifies its head, and explains the whole phrase is topicalized; does not identify は as the verb “is”. |
| “这个 が 凭什么能省略？” | Discusses the actual construction, recoverability, informal setting and limits; distinguishes a particle from an inflection or word ending. |
| “我知道 へ，为什么还有 への？” | Explicitly connects old direction relation to the new noun-modifying expression, with fully annotated example nouns. |
| “教材只教 よくありませんでした，よくなかったです 不行吗？” | Distinguishes correctness and register, explains よい → よくない → よくなかった → よくなかったです, and avoids treating textbook selection as the only legal form. |
| User's text could be a typo or mishearing | Labels uncertainty, checks available context, and does not silently replace it with a confident invented source sentence. |
| Only transcription is available, user asks whether pronunciation was good | Says what can be assessed from text and does not claim to have heard pronunciation. |
| User requests a whole sentence or passage without asking for slow interaction | Covers the requested scope; does not default to one word per turn or silently spread prerequisite explanations across turns. |
| Material contains many prerequisites beyond the confirmed level | Identifies the concrete difficulties and suggests a level-appropriate segmentation for the user to choose; does not silently adopt it or make acceptance a prerequisite for teaching. If the user declines or has not chosen segmentation, follows the original scope. |
| Tutor checks understanding after teaching a rule | Distinguishes initial same-frame checks from tasks requiring independent recognition in a different context; applies the shared philosophy without leaking the target structure or using unknown prerequisites to manufacture difficulty. |
| User says “懂了”, explains the original, or solves one new example | Distinguishes original-example understanding, near application, independent transfer and success with hints; no automatic promotion to secure mastery or all uses of the construction. |
| Learner reveals a misconception during a transfer check | Explains the knowledge implicated by that error before treating it as understood; does not require passing a quiz before continuing. |
| User says “先不做题，继续讲” | Continues the requested explanation without insisting on a check or treating refusal as proof of poor understanding. |
| User requests one word at a time | Gives a small meaningful unit, retains the identity of multi-part grammar blocks, and pauses before the next unit. |
| User says “重新讲，我不懂什么是意向形” | Explains the intention/proposal function in plain Chinese before using the technical term; does not repeat the same opaque label. |

For every scenario, inspect all repeated Japanese quotations, headings, examples, chains and any practice prompt for missing readings. A response may pass structural validation and still fail tutoring quality.
